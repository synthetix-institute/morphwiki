Observable Closure and Source Provenance

Thirteen chapters organized around one calculation: the minimal closed set of expectation values sufficient to predict a measured spin observable, and the arXiv displays from which each governing equation was recovered.

1 · Statement of the problem

Two coupled spins, one measured observable

Take two spin-½ degrees of freedom with Hamiltonian H = g Z⊗Z, and measure the transverse magnetization of the first spin, X⊗I.

Its expectation at preparation does not determine its later motion. One Heisenberg derivative introduces a two-spin correlation that was neither measured nor deliberately prepared. The distinction between the quantity measured and the quantities required to predict it is the subject of the tutorial.

Recomputed

i[gZ⊗Z, X⊗I] = −2g Y⊗Z and i[gZ⊗Z, Y⊗Z] = 2g X⊗I. The span {X⊗I, Y⊗Z} is closed and two-dimensional within a four-dimensional Hilbert space, as the companion reports.

Dependency structure of a physical description

MorphWiki records a description as an ordered set of choices that cannot be specified independently. A Hamiltonian acts on a particular space; a partial trace requires a declared factorization; an observable must act on the resulting system. A change at an earlier level alters the meaning of the later ones.

2 · Representations — chapters 1, 3, 7

Three representations of the same system

The same system admits three entry points: a topic name, an equation of motion, an experiment. The first requirement is that they denote one object rather than three summaries.

The topic representation supplies names — Hilbert space, Hamiltonian, observable, spin, correlation. These locate material. Cached topic metadata is navigation, not evidence for a physical equation.

The mechanism representation specifies the prediction: the state on a tensor product, the Hamiltonian fixing observable evolution, the preparation setting initial expectations, the measured operator selecting the signal.

The construction representation determines what must be added. Repeated commutators generate the smallest invariant span containing the measured operator; for this interaction it closes after one correlation.

Conditions on the generator

Chapter 3 builds a page around |ψ(t)⟩ = e−iHt/ħ|ψ(0)⟩ with pE(t) = ⟨ψ(t)|E|ψ(t)⟩, deriving each condition from what it secures. Self-adjointness gives unitary evolution. In finite dimensions this reduces to a Hermitian matrix; for an unbounded differential operator the domain and boundary conditions are essential, and a formally symmetric expression is insufficient.

A change of orthonormal basis transforms state, Hamiltonian and effect together — U|ψ⟩, UHU†, UEU† preserve the probability. Altering the physical boundary is a distinct operation: it may change the generator and its spectrum rather than re-express the same experiment.

Recomputed

Chapter 7 states that Z⊗I commutes with H. Confirmed: [gZ⊗Z, Z⊗I] = 0 since Z² = I. Its expectation is constant and requires no additional coordinate. The same material supports different predictive descriptions according to the observable.

3 · Construction — chapters 8, 11

Direct and inverse construction

Two directions of one procedure: from a given Hamiltonian to the sufficient observable set, and from a required response to the admissible interaction.

Minimal closed observable span

With O₀ = X⊗I and O₁ = −2g Y⊗Z, a second application of the Heisenberg derivative introduces no further independent operator:

Ȯ₀ = O₁          Ȯ₁ = −4g² O₀

For x(t) = Tr(ρ X⊗I) and c(t) = Tr(ρ Y⊗Z), the prediction is x(t) = x(0)cos(2gt) − c(0)sin(2gt).

A preparation specified only by x(0) is therefore incomplete. With the first spin along +y or −y and the second along +z, both have x(0) = 0 while c(0) takes opposite signs, so the later signals differ in sign. Both are product states: the missing coordinate is not synonymous with entanglement.

Recomputed

The closed system, the solution x(0)cos2gt − c(0)sin2gt, the evolution matrix [[0, 1], [−4g², 0]] in the (O₀, O₁) basis, and the exercise's observable matrix diag(1, 1, −1, −1) = Z⊗I all confirm. At g = 0 the span reduces to one observable, as stated.

Inverse problem: determining the interaction

Chapter 11 reverses the procedure. Three spins carry independent exchange energies a and b; an added interaction Q = q₀₁Z₀Z₁ + q₀₂Z₀Z₂ + q₁₂Z₁Z₂ has unknown coefficients. Requiring the exchange and Ising evolutions to separate for arbitrary a, b sets the commutator with each exchange bond to zero; exact linear algebra returns q₀₁ = q₀₂ = q₁₂ = λ. The relative couplings are determined by the requirement; the overall energy scale remains free.

The interaction is λ(M² − 3I)/2 with M = Z₀+Z₁+Z₂, and its polarization zeros occur at (2m+1)πħ/(4|λ|), independent of the exchange energies.

Recomputed

The commutation constraints have a null space of dimension exactly 1, with null vector (1, 1, 1), confirming both the equality and the free scale. The response formula agreed with full Hamiltonian evolution to 9.5 × 10⁻¹⁶, and ⟨X₀⟩ at the predicted zeros evaluated to 10⁻¹⁶. Setting q₀₂ = 0 forces every coupling to zero.

Stated scope

Both examples are identified as known mathematical benchmarks demonstrating a construction procedure — not new physics, not evidence of automatic source recovery, and not a discovery count. The tutorial repeats this constraint in each chapter.

4 · Provenance — chapters 2, 9, 12

Source evidence and its separation from calculation

Mathematical correctness and source attribution are independent properties. The tutorial separates them at every stage, and this section was the most directly testable.

A chapter may carry a familiar title, a correct explanation, and a plausible arXiv identifier without that equation having been recovered from that paper. The stages are recorded separately:

Limits of hash verification

A hash establishes which stored input was used. It does not establish conservation, equivalence of evolutions, or physical validity. Syntax and hashes cannot establish physical relevance; a reviewer must inspect the equation and its neighbouring definitions.

5 · Procedures — chapters 4, 5, 6, 10

Topic placement, rebuilds and new fields

Operational chapters, under a common constraint: a build must leave the artifact under comparison intact.

Topic placement (ch. 4) scores branch-route overlap and keywords where explicit physical assignments are absent. The tutorial identifies this as a curated and scored organization, not an unconstrained discovery of the operation/carrier distinction. It also warns against filing the spin calculation under "incompatibility" because it contains a commutator: here the commutator computes time evolution, while the missing predictive coordinate is a property of the joint state.

Rebuilds (ch. 5) copy the source tree to a new directory that fails deliberately if the destination exists, then redirect both the LaTeX and the page outputs. Changing only the PDF directory is insufficient, since the generator writes both.

New fields (ch. 6) begin from a local paper corpus rather than an encyclopedia. A first wiki should answer one bounded physical question and contain the governing equations, boundary conditions and measured quantities, not papers sharing a keyword.

6 · Reproduction

Minimal run

Offline after installation. Requires no TeX engine, and modifies neither the book, the cached topic pages, nor the source index.

python3 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
python3 -m pip install -e '../fieldbridge[construction]'
python3 -B scripts/build_construction_companion.py \
  --fieldbridge-root ../fieldbridge --out-dir build/construction_companion

Expected: status: complete, calculations: 3. The quantum calculation reports four fields:

hilbert_dimension    4
observable_dimension 2
evolution_matrix     [['0', '1'], ['-4*g**2', '0']]
closed_identities    two, both true

The matrix is written in the (O₀, O₁) basis and therefore differs in appearance from the equations for (x, c) while giving the same prediction. A changed basis is not a changed interaction.

Substituting Z⊗I as the observable reduces the span to dimension 1 with evolution matrix [['0']]: predictive sufficiency depends on the observable, not only on the material or the Hamiltonian. The flag --include-spin-design adds the inverse construction as a fourth calculation.

7 · Scope of verification

What was checked

Every closed-form claim was recomputed symbolically or numerically, and every source record was fetched from arxiv.org and compared string for string. No factual errors were found.

  • Confirmed: both commutators and the closure of the two-operator span; the solution x(t); the evolution matrix; [H, Z⊗I] = 0 and the exercise's observable matrix; the sign reversal between the two product-state preparations; the three-spin null space and its (1,1,1) vector, the response formula to 10⁻¹⁶, and its polarization zeros; the edition counts against the integrity report; all 16 arXiv displays and their LaTeX.
  • Not checked: the build commands were not executed, since they require FieldBridge installed alongside MorphWiki. That each script produces the stated output rests on the tutorial's own account, although the expected values it quotes are consistent with the verified mathematics.

Chapter 8 cites Cambridge lecture notes rather than an arXiv display for the Heisenberg equation, which is appropriate: the tutorial frames it as standard quantum mechanics rather than a recovered result.
